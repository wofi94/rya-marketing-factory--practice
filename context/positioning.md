# Positioning

Rya Rightsizing is the compassionate local partner for rightsizing and moving in North and West Vancouver and the Sea to Sky corridor. We take the logistics and the emotional weight off the people involved, and we treat a lifetime of belongings as a story to honour, not a load to clear. Our work serves three audiences, and each hears the same promise in a slightly different way.

Our main contact is the adult child, who is usually the first to reach out. Families in North and West Vancouver tend to be close to their parents, so distance is rarely the issue. The real pressures are the workload that comes with decades of accumulated belongings, and the emotional strain of watching a parent struggle to let go. We position Rya as the service that takes that load off their plate. We handle the sorting, donations and moving, and act as a single trusted liaison, so the adult child gets relief and a clear plan. We also make clear that their parent stays in control of every decision and that nothing is taken from them.

Seniors are the people at the centre of the move. Many want to stay in their home, feel capable of caring for it, and keep the things that matter most. They may have a lot of “absolutes,” and our role is to work through them gently, at their pace. We guide rather than decide. We reassure them that a cherished item can find a good next home, whether with their children, through a local donation or in a place where it will be better used, so they remain in control of their home and belongings through every step.

Referral partners, such as estate lawyers, social workers and realtors, care most about reliability. For them, Rya is the dependable local specialist for the clearing and transition work their clients need. We keep families informed, document what goes where for executors, and treat every senior with dignity and respect, so partners can refer with confidence. We also want to be recognized as experts in this space, which we show through the founder’s experience, her community board work and clear guidance on the rightsizing process.

Instructions for using this positioning

Any document, post, email, ad or page produced from this context must keep the positioning intact. Apply the following every time.

Lead with the right audience. Identify which audience the piece is for (adult child, senior or referral partner) and write to that person directly. The adult child is the default primary audience unless told otherwise.
Keep the core promise in every piece. Each piece should make clear that Rya takes the logistics and emotional weight off the people involved, and that belongings are treated as a story to honour, not a load to clear.
Always protect the senior’s control. Never suggest that Rya decides for the senior, takes things from them or rushes them. Rya guides, and the senior and family decide. Use language such as “at your pace” and “you stay in control.”
Lead with workload and emotional support for adult children. Do not build messaging around distance. For North and West Vancouver families, the pressures are workload and the emotional strain of a parent letting go.
Frame belongings as having a good next home. Reassure rather than pressure. A cherished item can go to family, a local donation or a better use.
Speak to reliability for referral partners. Emphasize dependability, clear communication with families, documentation of what goes where, and dignity for the senior. Show expertise through the founder’s experience, community board work and practical guidance, not through unsupported claims.
Use the approved language. Say “rightsizing” and do not use the word “downsizing.” Use “we” for Rya and “you” for the reader. Keep the tone compassionate, reassuring, respectful, clear and hopeful. Do not use fear, urgency or guilt to sell.
Do not invent. Do not add prices, services, credentials, testimonials, statistics or results that are not confirmed. Mark any gap as [NEEDS CLIENT INPUT: ...].
Run a final check before finishing. Ask: Does this piece speak to one clear audience? Does it ease the workload or protect the senior’s control? Does it treat belongings with respect? Does it avoid “downsizing”? If any answer is no, revise before delivering.
