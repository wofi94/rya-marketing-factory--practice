## How to use this document

Use it before you write, review or approve any marketing or communication content for Rya Rightsizing: blog posts, social posts, emails, B2B materials, ads, video, scripts, web pages.

**Rule of thumb:** if a fact is not in this document or the Brand Book, do not say it. Do not soften it, and do not swap in a similar claim. Leave it out, and in a draft mark the gap as `[NEEDS CLIENT INPUT: ...]`.

Every rule has a source tag so you can tell where it came from:

| Tag | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `[Brand Book]` | Stated in the Brand Book |
| `[Lisa]` | Said by Lisa Fisher in the interview |
| `[Team]` | Decided by the project team. Not stated by Lisa |
| `[Practice]` | Recommended good practice. Not a requirement from Lisa or the Brand Book |

Each section sorts rules into **Allowed**, **Not allowed**, and **Needs evidence, permission or approval**.

If this document and the Brand Book ever conflict, stop and flag it. Do not pick one.

**Approval:** Lisa Fisher, as company owner, approves anything that needs approval below. `[Practice]`

## 4. Audience sensitivity

### Who we speak to

- **Primary:** adult children, often busy or far away. `[Brand Book]` `[Lisa]`
- **Secondary:** the seniors themselves. `[Brand Book]` `[Lisa]`
- **Also:** families clearing a loved one's home, and professionals who meet seniors (social workers, discharge nurses, lawyers, funeral homes, realtors). `[Brand Book]` `[Lisa]`

Voice and tone have their own document. Follow it. Until it is in the repository, Brand Book sections 6 to 8 apply. This section covers only what the guardrails add.

### Allowed

- Say clearly that **the senior decides what stays and what goes.** `[Lisa]`
  *Example wording only, not approved copy: "Your pace, your plan. You decide what stays."*
- Frame letting go as keeping what matters in this chapter, with belongings given a new life. `[Brand Book]` `[Lisa]`
  *Example wording only, not approved copy: "Keep the memory. Let the item live a new life."*
- Name hard situations **calmly** in educational content (a hospital stay, a fall, a partner's passing, memory changes) when families search for help at those moments. Describe the family's situation, not a diagnosis. Never use the situation as a threat. `[Lisa]` `[Practice]`
- Use "you are not alone" and "no judgment" language. `[Lisa]`
- Speak to seniors who may have no involved family. Lisa described seniors who are alone, whose children live far away, or who are estranged. `[Lisa]`
- Give one clear next step, with no urgency and no preparation tasks. People who are overwhelmed often cannot make the first call. `[Lisa]` `[Brand Book]`

### Not allowed

- **Death framing:** "last step," "end of the road," "final chapter," "before it's too late." Lisa said many seniors see rightsizing as the last step before they die. `[Lisa]` `[Brand Book]`
- **Loss framing:** "get rid of," "purge," "clear out the house," "junk" about belongings. `[Brand Book]`
- **Overriding the senior:** copy that implies Rya or the family decides for them, such as "we'll handle everything for your parent." Use "with" language. `[Lisa]` `[Brand Book]`
- **Labels:** "elderly," "old folks," "hoarder," "cluttered mess." `[Brand Book]`
- **Stereotypes:** seniors shown as frail, helpless or confused, as a burden, or as unable to use technology. Where Lisa reaches seniors on Facebook and in print, that is a channel choice, not a trait. `[Brand Book]` `[Practice]`
- **Guilt or blame toward the adult child:** "you should have done this sooner," "your parent can't cope alone." Adult children are often guilty and stretched already. `[Brand Book]` `[Practice]`
- **Wealth and property signals** in public copy ("for homeowners with large homes," "affluent seniors"). Who Rya hopes to reach is internal. `[Practice]`
- **Assuming family:** do not assume the senior has children, that they live nearby, or that they get along. `[Lisa]`
- **Cheerful or salesy tone** in grief content. Use the Brand Book "quiet, tender, patient" tone. `[Brand Book]`

### Needs approval

- Any wording that goes beyond the example phrases above for the senior's decision-making, or for hard situations. `[Practice]`
